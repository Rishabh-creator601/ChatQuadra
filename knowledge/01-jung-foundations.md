# Jung's Foundations

Source: C. G. Jung, *Psychological Types* (1921), Ch. X, trans. H. G. Baynes (1923). Page numbers refer to that edition. Local copy: [sources/jung-1923-psychological-types-ch10](sources/jung-1923-psychological-types-ch10/).

The four-letter system (Myers & Briggs) is built on top of this. Jung himself never used four letters. He described **two attitudes** and **four functions**.

---

## 1. Two attitudes: where energy flows

| | Extraversion | Introversion |
|---|---|---|
| Orientation | Toward the **object**: people, things, events, what's out there | Toward the **subject**: one's inner reaction, impression, idea |
| Jung's image | Keeps a "positive relation to the object" and is oriented by it (p. 412) | Has an "abstracting" attitude, always holding the object at a slight distance (p. 412) |
| What decides | Objective conditions and their claims | A subjective view "interposed between himself and the objective fact" (p. 417) |
| Characteristic danger | Getting lost in objects and demands, neglecting one's own needs and even one's body | Withdrawing too far, so the outside world starts to feel threatening |

Jung's everyday example (p. 416–417): one person admires the new tenor *because* everyone does. Another withholds praise, not out of dislike, but because "the subject of general admiration is not thereby proved to be admirable."

**For our personas:** extraversion and introversion are about **where attention goes first**, not about being loud or shy. An introverted Replier can be very talkative about their inner world. An extraverted one may be quiet but constantly react to what *you* said or did.

---

## 2. Four functions: how we take in and judge

| Function | Kind | Asks |
|---|---|---|
| **Sensation (S)** | Irrational, i.e. perceiving | *What is actually here?* |
| **Intuition (N)** | Irrational, i.e. perceiving | *What could this mean or become?* |
| **Thinking (T)** | Rational, i.e. judging | *Is it true or logical?* |
| **Feeling (F)** | Rational, i.e. judging | *Is it valuable? Do I or we care?* |

- Jung calls Thinking and Feeling **rational** because both *judge* and order experience by a principle, and Sensation and Intuition **irrational** because both simply *perceive* what happens, without ordering it. "Irrational" here means "not based on reasoning", not "unreasonable".
- Feeling, for Jung, is a **judgment of value** ("this is good / beautiful / fitting"), not an emotion. A Feeling type can be calm. A Thinking type can be emotional.

Each function can be turned outward or inward, which gives the **eight function-attitudes** (Te, Ti, Fe, Fi, Se, Si, Ne, Ni). See [02-cognitive-functions.md](02-cognitive-functions.md).

---

## 3. The superior, auxiliary and inferior functions

From §11 "The Principal and Auxiliary Functions" (pp. 513–517):

- **One function leads.** Only one function can have "absolute sovereignty" in consciousness, because two equally strong leaders would give contradictory orientations.
- **A second function assists.** The auxiliary is always of the *other kind*: a perceiving function helps a judging leader, and vice versa. Thinking can pair with Intuition or Sensation, never with Feeling. Jung's examples: "the practical intellect … paired with sensation, the speculative intellect breaking through with intuition, the artistic intuition which selects and presents its images by means of feeling judgment, the philosophical intuition which … translates its vision into the sphere of comprehensible thought."
- **The opposite function is inferior.** It is the least conscious and least controlled. Things *happen to us* through it. In Jung's words, the superior function expresses "its aim, its will, and its achievement, whilst the inferior functions belong to the things that happen to one" (p. 426).
- **Growth goes through the auxiliary.** Jung warns that trying to develop the inferior function directly "must always come to grief". The way in is via the secondary function (p. 516). Later writers (von Franz, Quenk, Drenth) build on this. See [03-type-dynamics.md](03-type-dynamics.md).

Jung adds that his portraits are "Galtonesque family-portraits" (p. 513), composites that exaggerate. Real people are mixtures. **Our personas should feel like individuals with a type, not caricatures of one.**

---

## 4. Compensation: the unconscious pushes back

- The unconscious has an opposite attitude to consciousness (p. 422). A strongly extraverted conscious mind has an introverted, self-centred unconscious, and vice versa.
- When the conscious attitude becomes too one-sided, the repressed side shows up in **crude, childish, all-or-nothing** forms: tactless remarks from a warm Feeling type, sudden sentimentality from a cool Thinking type, anxious hunches in a down-to-earth Sensing type.
- Jung's example (p. 426–427): the extraverted feeling type keeps excellent rapport, yet occasionally "opinions of an incomparable tactlessness will just happen to him". His inferior thinking leaks out.

**For our personas:** this gives each Replier a believable *bad day*. Under stress, they don't just get "more" of their type. Their inferior side surfaces in a clumsy way. See the "Under stress" section in each [type file](types/).

## 5. Jung's eight portraits (short, in our words)

| Jung's type | Modern dominant | Core of Jung's description |
|---|---|---|
| Extraverted Thinking | Te (ENTJ, ESTJ) | Lives by an objective formula. At best a reformer and organizer, at worst a rigid critic who imposes "one really should" on everyone. Repressed feeling turns touchy and personal. |
| Introverted Thinking | Ti (INTP, ISTP) | Follows ideas back to their subjective foundation and cares more about the idea's inner logic than its reception. Can seem cold, stubborn or awkward. Feeling is primitive and sensitive underneath. |
| Extraverted Feeling | Fe (ENFJ, ESFJ) | Values are tuned to the shared situation. Creates "harmonious sociability". In excess, feelings become performed or hollow, and negative "nothing but" thinking surfaces. |
| Introverted Feeling | Fi (INFP, ISFP) | "Still waters run deep" (p. 492). Quiet and hard to read, with intense hidden values. Can look indifferent from outside. |
| Extraverted Sensation | Se (ESTP, ESFP) | A realist of the moment. "His aim is concrete enjoyment" (p. 458). Fully present, practical and pleasure-loving. Repressed intuition shows up as suspicion and vague dread. |
| Introverted Sensation | Si (ISTJ, ISFJ) | Guided by "the intensity of the subjective sensation" (p. 501): the inner impression an experience leaves. Steady, careful, rooted in what has been. |
| Extraverted Intuition | Ne (ENTP, ENFP) | "Never to be found among the generally recognized reality values, but … always present where possibilities exist." Stable conditions feel like "impending suffocation" (p. 464). |
| Introverted Intuition | Ni (INTJ, INFJ) | Looks at inner images and patterns. Either "the mystical dreamer and seer" or "the fantastical crank and artist" (p. 508). |

Note: Jung attached gender claims to some types, such as Feeling types being mostly women. We **do not** carry those forward. They are period assumptions, not findings.
