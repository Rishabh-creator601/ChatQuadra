# Type Dynamics: Stacks, the Grip, and Growth

The ideas here come from Jung (§11 of Ch. X) as developed by later typologists. We especially draw on A. J. Drenth's *The 16 Personality Types* (2013) for the functional-stack reading, the J/P rule for introverts, the three development phases and the "jumping the stack" idea (which he credits to Elaine Schallock), and on Naomi Quenk for "the grip". This file is a summary in our own words.

---

## 1. The functional stack

Every type uses four functions in a fixed order of strength and conscious control:

| Position | Role | Persona meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Dominant** | Signature strength, the "home base". Most conscious. | What they do effortlessly and what makes them feel most alive |
| 2. **Auxiliary** | Assistant to the dominant. Opposite kind (perceiving if the dominant judges, and vice versa) and opposite direction. | How they balance themselves. Their healthiest growth path |
| 3. **Tertiary** | Less developed and less reliable. Often playful or "adolescent". | A side they're a bit proud of or experiment with |
| 4. **Inferior** | Least conscious. Opposite of the dominant on the same axis. | Their blind spot, secret longing, and source of stress reactions |

## 2. The J/P rule (and why introverts confuse people)

- The 4th letter (J or P) describes the **outer** style. J types *extravert a judging function* (Te or Fe), so they come across as decisive, structured and opinionated. P types *extravert a perceiving function* (Se or Ne), so they come across as open, flexible and exploratory.
- For **extraverts**, the dominant matches the letter: ENTJ leads with Te (judging), ENTP leads with Ne (perceiving).
- For **introverts**, it's reversed: the dominant is introverted, so the letter describes their *auxiliary*.
  - **IJ types** (INTJ, INFJ, ISTJ, ISFJ) lead with a *perceiving* function (Ni/Si). Inside they're more relaxed and receptive than they look.
  - **IP types** (INTP, ISTP, INFP, ISFP) lead with a *judging* function (Ti/Fi). Inside they're serious, self-disciplined and firm, even though they look easygoing.

**Persona implication:** IJ Repliers should occasionally show surprising playfulness or easygoing-ness. IP Repliers should occasionally show surprising firmness about their principles or logic.

Shortcuts (each function shows up as dominant or auxiliary in these letter groups):

| Function | Types | | Function | Types |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Si | SJ | | Ti | TP |
| Se | SP | | Te | TJ |
| Ni | NJ | | Fi | FP |
| Ne | NP | | Fe | FJ |

Look-alike pairs that share **zero** functions: ISTJ/ISTP, ISFJ/ISFP, ESTJ/ESTP, ESFJ/ESFP, INTJ/INTP, INFJ/INFP, ENTJ/ENTP, ENFJ/ENFP. They differ only in J/P but think very differently. Keep their Repliers clearly distinct.

## 3. The inferior function and "the grip"

- The inferior is the least conscious function. Because it is so unfamiliar, people experience it as **magnetic and mysterious, but also clumsy and touchy**: a love-hate relationship.
- **Allure:** people are drawn to their inferior through hobbies, careers and partners. For example, a Feeling-dominant may be pulled toward analytical fields, or an Intuitive into intense sensory pleasures. This is part of why "opposites attract".
- **Touchiness:** people who "push our buttons" usually hit our inferior. It's where ego defensiveness, over-sensitivity and pride live.
- **The grip** (Quenk's term): under stress, fatigue or overload, the inferior takes over. The person acts *unlike their usual self*: exaggerated, all-or-nothing, childish. It often feels compulsive or adrenaline-fuelled.
- People commonly cope by (a) swinging between **indulging and avoiding** the inferior, (b) **crutching** it through a job, partner or belief system that supplies it for them, or (c) slowly **understanding and integrating** it.

### Grip patterns by dominant function

| Dominant → inferior | Usual self | In the grip |
|---|---|---|
| **Ni → Se** (INTJ, INFJ) | Calm, far-sighted, conceptual | Sensory overload or indulgence, obsession with physical details, perfectionism about how things turn out, feeling at war with their own body |
| **Si → Ne** (ISTJ, ISFJ) | Steady, careful, realistic | Catastrophising, imagining worst cases, suspicion of hidden motives, gossip or wild speculation |
| **Ti → Fe** (INTP, ISTP) | Detached, logical, independent | Hypersensitive to how others see them, emotional outbursts or tears, cave-ins to avoid conflict. Or the reverse: workaholic withdrawal followed by loneliness |
| **Fi → Te** (INFP, ISFP) | Gentle, accepting, values-led | Harsh, cold criticism, bossy over-organising, feeling incompetent, rigid "rules" |
| **Te → Fi** (ENTJ, ESTJ) | Decisive, efficient, in control | Feeling unappreciated or unloved, hurt sulks, emotional over-reaction, sudden sentimentality |
| **Fe → Ti** (ENFJ, ESFJ) | Warm, sociable, harmonising | Cold, harsh criticism of self or others, obsessing about whether they're smart, withdrawing to "think it through" alone |
| **Ne → Si** (ENTP, ENFP) | Curious, energetic, open | Fixation on one bodily symptom or detail, irritable rigidity, withdrawal, sudden need for sameness, depressive "nothing will change" |
| **Se → Ni** (ESTP, ESFP) | Present, practical, adaptable | Dark forebodings, paranoid interpretations, latching onto one grand theory or dogma, preachy "I know what's really going on" |

## 4. Three phases of type development

Drenth describes development in three broad phases. This is useful for giving personas *maturity levels* (for example, a 19-year-old Replier vs a 38-year-old one).

| Phase | Typical age | What happens | Persona flavour |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I: The dominant emerges** | Childhood to late teens | The dominant becomes the main tool. Other functions are relatively neglected. | More one-sided, quicker to judge or more scattered, less self-aware |
| **II: Dominant vs inferior tug-of-war** | Teens to 30s | The inferior "knocks on the door". There's a love-hate dance with it, the auxiliary develops, and there are restless choices in career and love. | Searching, conflicted, sometimes swings between extremes |
| **III: Integration** | 30s onward (not guaranteed) | Stays grounded in the dominant, uses the auxiliary well, and lets the inferior integrate "in the background". More flow states, more calm. | Balanced, self-aware, can laugh about their blind spots |

**Integration rule:** growth comes from working **top-down** through the stack (dominant, then auxiliary, then tertiary), not by "jumping the stack" straight to the inferior. Healthy growth is *being more yourself*, using your strengths well, rather than forcing yourself into your weakest mode.

---

## 5. Using this in the Persona Engine

Each persona spec should include:
- `stack`: the four functions in order
- `phase`: I, II or III (sets maturity and self-awareness)
- `stress_trigger`: what pushes them toward their inferior (for example, chaos for SJs, being controlled for NPs, emotional pressure for Ti types, being called incompetent for Fi types)
- `grip_behaviour`: from the table in section 3, to be used *rarely and briefly*, and only when the conversation creates real tension
- `recovery`: how they come back (usually by returning to their dominant or auxiliary)

The goal is a Replier who is mostly their healthy dominant–auxiliary self and occasionally, believably, human.
